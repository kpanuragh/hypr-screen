Hypr Screen turns your Android phone into a real extra monitor for the <b>Hyprland</b> Wayland compositor on Linux, a bit like Apple Sidecar.

<b>Features</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Extended display, not a mirror:</b> Hyprland gets a real monitor at your phone's resolution, with its own workspaces.</li>
<li><b>Plug and play over USB:</b> plug the phone in and it becomes a monitor. Unplug it and the monitor goes away.</li>
<li><b>Wi-Fi:</b> pair once by typing a 6-digit code on the laptop, then connect without a cable.</li>
<li><b>Touch:</b> tap, drag and scroll on the phone.</li>
<li><b>Keyboard:</b> type with the phone keyboard. An extra row gives Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, Super and arrow keys. A Bluetooth keyboard paired to the phone works too.</li>
<li><b>Low latency:</b> hardware H.264 encoding on the laptop and hardware decoding on the phone.</li>
<li><b>Secure:</b> every connection uses TLS 1.3 with a pinned certificate and a per-device pairing key.</li>
</ul>

<b>Requirements</b>
This app is the phone half. Your Linux computer needs the hypr-screen service (Hyprland 0.56+, wf-recorder, adb, Python 3). See the project page for one-command install instructions.

No accounts, ads, analytics or Google services. The app only talks to your own computer.
